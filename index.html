Course Learning Snapshot

What you can claim

DESN 374 was not only about trying AI tools. The professional skill is learning how to direct, evaluate, document, and communicate AI-assisted design work.

Model Literacy

Compared frontier models for design tasks

You tested how different models respond, where they help, and where they need human correction.

Prompt + Workflow Design

Turned vague goals into usable instructions

You practiced context, constraints, examples, iteration, and model-specific prompting.

Generative Media Direction

Used AI tools with design judgment

You explored image, layout, editing, and tool workflows while deciding what served the project.

Research + Documentation

Built evidence around process

You used research notes, visual notes, Milanote, model comparisons, websites, and reflections.

02

The 4D AI Fluency Frame

Use these words
Move Plain meaning Resume language
Delegation Deciding what to ask AI to do, when to use it, and what to keep human. Scoped AI assistance for research, ideation, drafting, critique, and production tasks.
Description Giving models clear context, constraints, examples, and output expectations. Designed prompts and workflow instructions for model-assisted design exploration.
Discernment Judging what is useful, false, generic, biased, off-brand, or worth revising. Evaluated AI outputs for quality, relevance, feasibility, and alignment with design goals.
Diligence Owning the result, checking sources, documenting process, and revising responsibly. Documented AI-assisted decisions, iterations, limitations, and final design rationale.

07

Portfolio Case Study

Tell the project story

A strong case study is not "here is what the AI made." It shows your question, your process, your decisions, your outcome, and what you would do next.

01 Context

What were you investigating, making, or trying to understand?

02 Method

Which tools, models, sources, prompts, or workflows shaped the work?

03 Process

What did you try, change, reject, compare, document, or revise?

04 Outcome

What artifact, prototype, website, system, presentation, or research result can someone inspect?

05 Reflection

What did you learn about AI, design judgment, limits, audience, or your own working style?

08

Interview Language

Say it clearly
What did you use AI for?

I used AI for [research/ideation/prototyping/critique/drafting], but I was responsible for the brief, constraints, evaluation, revisions, and final decisions.

How did you evaluate AI outputs?

I checked whether outputs matched the goal, audience, visual direction, source evidence, and constraints. I also documented weak results because they showed what needed to change.

What did you actually build?

I built [artifact/prototype/workflow/site], supported by [research/process evidence]. The important part was not just the output, but the documented path from question to decision.

What would you improve next?

I would improve [specific part] by testing with [audience/tool/source], tightening [constraint], or extending [feature/process] based on what I learned.

Evidence Checklist

Back up each claim

Responsible wording rule

Use "AI-assisted" when AI helped. Use "designed," "directed," "evaluated," "documented," or "revised" when you made the human decisions. Do not hide the tool, and do not give the tool credit for your judgment.
